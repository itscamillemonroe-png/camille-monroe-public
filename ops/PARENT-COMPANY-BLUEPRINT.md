## 1. Parent-company doctrine

Camille Monroe is one founder-controlled parent operating umbrella with multiple divisions, revenue lanes, target markets, and sub-brands.

The company is built around one priority:

**Convert legitimate opportunity into collected cash as quickly as practical while building recurring revenue, owned IP, relationship capital, and transferable business assets.**

Every opportunity is ranked by:

1. Brand-culture fit.
2. Time to **collected** cash.
3. Probability of conversion/payment.
4. Upfront cash required.
5. Gross margin.
6. Founder workload.
7. Repeatability/recurring potential.
8. Scalability.
9. Relationship/IP value.
10. Legal, licensing, disclosure, privacy, and reputation risk.

Pipeline value is never treated as revenue until money clears.

## 2. What counts as a division vs. a target market

A **division** owns a repeatable capability, operating system, offer family, or asset class.

A **target market / vertical** is an audience that one or more divisions can serve.

This prevents Camille Monroe from becoming an unstructured “everything company.”

### Core target verticals

- Beauty & lifestyle.
- Creators / influencers / personal brands.
- Hospitality, travel, venues, and nightlife.
- Events, festivals, and premium local vendors.
- Creator agencies / management companies.
- Small businesses with strong lifestyle or experience positioning.

Beauty, hospitality, and events are therefore normally **markets inside PR/Partnerships**, not separate parent-company divisions.

## 11. Cross-company cash-conversion engines

Every division can use one or more of these economics:

### Engine 1 — Existing compensation
Affiliate, referral, commission, creator campaign, vendor incentive, or other economics already established.

### Engine 2 — Urgent demand
A buyer already has a time-sensitive problem. Package a defined result, price it, collect, then fulfill.

### Engine 3 — Created economics / cold outreach
Camille Monroe identifies value first, creates an offer, establishes written economics, then delivers.

### Engine 4 — Subcontracting margin
Camille Monroe owns the client relationship and project management while qualified contractors perform outside-normal-operation work.

### Engine 5 — Recurring revenue
Memberships, retainers, support agreements, subscriptions, repeat commissions, licensing renewals, and ongoing management.

### Engine 6 — Owned-IP leverage
Create once, sell/license repeatedly: software, templates, collectibles, content/IP, workflows, and brand assets.

## 12. Subcontracting doctrine

Founder default operating role is digital, strategic, written, and email-first.

Subcontract by default for:

- field work,
- live event staffing,
- photography/video capture,
- physical fulfillment,
- phone-heavy execution,
- delivery/logistics,
- specialist production,
- technical implementation outside the approved founder workflow.

Track:

- client price,
- contractor cost,
- expected margin,
- scope,
- deliverables,
- deadline,
- rights,
- confidentiality,
- payment status.

Prefer collecting client funds/deposit before incurring contractor expense.
